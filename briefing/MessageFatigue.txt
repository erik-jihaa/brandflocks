AI-generated text on social platforms has created a paradox: the writing is often technically flawless, but it actively repels the people reading it.
The root of the problem isn't just about the words themselves—it's about the erosion of human connection in spaces originally built for exactly that.
The Core Problem: The Authenticity Deficit
When users scroll through social media, they are looking for presence, lived experiences, and a distinct point of view. The unchecked proliferation of AI content replaces that with what researchers call "overproduction"—endlessly polished, frictionless content designed to capture attention rather than express anything meaningful.
This creates a phenomenon known as message fatigue. As feeds fill up with synthetic text, users increasingly question the reality of everything they read, leading to a general distrust of the digital ecosystem. People begin to feel that they are reading content generated by a machine, posted by a bot, to be read by other bots.
How People Perceive AI Content
Humans actually struggle to identify AI text in blind tests—often rating it as equal to or better than human writing. However, the moment they suspect or know it's AI, their perception drastically changes:
	•	The Trust Cliff: Studies show that when AI authorship is disclosed (or strongly suspected), perceived trustworthiness, competence, and likability nose-dive by up to 20%.
	•	The "Consciousness Gap": People accept AI for informational tasks, but reject it for emotional or persuasive ones. When an AI tries to motivate you or express empathy, it triggers a "legitimacy violation." The reader knows the machine has no actual feelings or stakes in the matter, so the attempt at connection feels manipulative.
	•	The "Uncanny Valley" of Style: Readers easily spot the stylistic fingerprints of AI. They perceive the writing as overly safe, neutral, and lacking a soul. The relentless, staccato rhythm of short sentences and one-sentence paragraphs leaves readers vaguely fatigued.
	•	Buzzword Fatigue: Words like delve, leverage, testament, seamless, and holistic have become massive red flags. Readers perceive these as lazy filler that takes up space without adding meaning.
How to Make It Better
You don't have to abandon AI, but you do need to shift it from the "front stage" to the "backstage." Use AI for outlining, research, and structure, but let human intent handle the final delivery.
When editing AI-generated drafts, apply these fixes:
	1	Inject Real Stakes and Stories: AI cannot replicate lived experience. Add specific metrics, personal anecdotes, or the exact moment you learned the lesson you're sharing. Prove you have skin in the game.
	2	Break the AI Rhythm: AI writes with a predictable, monotonous cadence. Intentionally mix wild variations in sentence length—follow a sprawling, complex 30-word sentence with a punchy 4-word fragment.
	3	Purge the Clichés: Create a blacklist of AI buzzwords. Replace generic, oily verbs (like explore or ensure) with sharp, specific language.
	4	Add Friction and Opinion: AI defaults to a "neutral, safe" consensus. Challenge conventional wisdom. Take a firm stance or inject a slightly controversial opinion that sparks actual debate.
	5	Calm Down the Formatting: AI loves to summarize everything into bullet points and overuse emojis. Turn choppy lists back into flowing, narrative paragraphs unless you are writing a strict step-by-step tutorial.
The ultimate goal isn't just to trick people into thinking a human wrote it—it's to actually provide the human value that AI fundamentally lacks.

The Editing Strategy
To achieve this transformation yourself, look at the AI output as raw research. Extract the core message—in this case, "good leaders care about their people during a crisis"—and throw away every single sentence the AI wrote. Rebuild the thought using a specific memory, a messy truth, or a blunt opinion.
